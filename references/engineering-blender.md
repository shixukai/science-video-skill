# 3D animation production: Blender and 3D asset reuse

3D-animation-style videos use **Blender** or agent-selected **[`vork`](tool-routing.md#vork-execution)** for nature, everyday-life, engineering, industrial and manufacturing topics: unless the user explicitly specifies an engine, the agent chooses Blender for multi-part mechanical CAD/mesh topology, boolean/modifier cutaways, non-rigid deformations, armatures, character rigging/lip-sync and `.blend` library reuse, and chooses `vork` (for the full episode or selected shots) when invisible physical fields/waves, thermal/concentration gradients, Slang GPU simulations, custom shaders or clean code-authored parametric/`glTF` studio mechanisms express the phenomenon more directly. Reuse existing projects and 3D assets, register `controlled_3d`, and do not require a video-model trial first. Photorealistic 3D also belongs to this route; AI-generated 3D-looking footage or MoneyPrinterTurbo montage cannot replace controlled 3D rendering. Realistic-style work uses MoneyPrinterTurbo under [style-based routing](tool-routing.md#lock-the-primary-tool-by-final-film-style); engineering topics do not automatically select 3D style.

Engineering examples check only components applicable to this topic; reuse supporting narration, subtitle, mixing and encoding tools without changing the main Blender route. Apply [proportionate acceptance](quality-acceptance.md#risk-based-rechecks-and-evidence-reuse): necessary visible/explanatory structure is mandatory, while engineering tolerances and exhaustive hidden assemblies do not become implicit video requirements. Review principal objects and key action states together using existing blocking/shot evidence before polishing small additions. Optional unrequested aids may be simplified or omitted when they add no understanding; preserve the requested meaning and any explicitly required effect.

Organize the complete engineering explanation under [full chain and duration](topic-contract.md#complete-chains-and-duration-for-engineering-and-manufacturing-topics) before planning whole-object, local and process shots in 3D. Local modeling detail cannot replace explaining upstream/downstream stages, manufacturing or system results.

## Scope of asset-only tasks

For model, static-preview, retrieval or cataloging commissions alone, deliver the relevant editable asset and inspection/archive results: check source models, scientific structure, connections/orientation, required views and dependencies; check action only when motion is involved. With no audio-track purpose, do not invent narration, a full-episode shot plan or G3. Modeling within a complete video remains intermediate work with subsequent audiovisual production and acceptance obligations.

## Recognize the actual object before explaining its interior

- Research public real-object photographs, sectional views and reliable technical information; first check silhouette, key components, relative scale and spatial relationships. A modern airliner engine cannot be identified by a cylinder and repeated blades alone: retain fan, nacelle, bypass/core-flow relationships as the topic requires before local refinement. When the topic explains an internal mechanism, sub-assembly or component inside a larger vehicle, machine, appliance or installation, make the host system unmistakable in the 0–5 s opening—either via the complete host or via an immediately recognizable functional host space/cabin/bay (such as a driver's cockpit with steering wheel, seat, belt path and pillar)—and bridge in one unbroken single-take move or simultaneous cutaway into the mechanism's real mounting site and relative scale before isolating internals; do not retreat to a simplified distant exterior shell when a functional cabin/bay already establishes clear host orientation at closer scale, and never open on a disembodied studio rig or unrecognizable fragment. Do not mandate the same model, stage count or parts list for every subject.
- Public product information can guide exterior form; it does not make a self-built model manufacturer-official CAD, a precise maintenance drawing or a validated flow field. Distinguish resemblance, teaching geometry and engineering simulation, keeping sources, rights and applicability limits in [science and asset records](science-and-rights.md).
- Check necessary structures in whole-object, medium and mechanism close-ups before surface polish. Give pipes, cables and passages real endpoints and interfaces. Distinguish materials through appropriate roughness and reflection response. For metallic mechanisms, controlled broad reflections can reveal curvature, edges and separations; adjust light size and direction so glare does not erase the interfaces being explained. Check these relationships at the target viewing size. Surface detail cannot conceal missing structural connections.

## High-poly industrial modeling standard for Colab 3D

Unless the user commissions a conflicting visual style, Colab-rendered 3D Blender scenes default to **high-poly industrial style (`high_poly_industrial_3d`, modeling profile `high_poly_industrial_v1`)**: precision CNC / CAD hardware and mechanical teardown fidelity (Apple Keynote hardware teardown, Branch Education precision mechanism, and industrial product photography caliber) across whole assemblies, cross-sectional cutaways, exploded views, macro mechanism close-ups, and grounded industrial test benches. Never deliver low-poly faceted cylinders, razor-sharp unchamfered cubes, single-sided zero-thickness cutaway shells, bare toy primitives, or chunky textbook arrows.

1. **Curvature and high-poly silhouette density (zero low-poly faceting).** Build hero curved silhouettes so no polygonal faceting is visible at the closest planned camera distance:
   - Hero cylinders, turned shafts, collars, housings, bezels and vessels: `>= 64` radial segments (`96–128` segments for macro close-up hero rings, lens barrels, bearings, or optical glass cylinders).
   - Secondary visible structural rods, fasteners, ports and pins in medium/close shots: `>= 32–48` segments (never default 16-segment or 24-segment polygonal pipes in hero views).
   - Flexible hoses, conduits, helical springs, straps and curved fillets: high-resolution splines (`resolution_u >= 24`, `bevel_resolution >= 8`) or `Subdivision Surface` (render level `>= 2`) with supporting holding loops.
   - Start from verified open-licensed high-poly CAD/glTF models (`CC0`/`CC-BY`, e.g. Poly Haven or Khronos) paired with 1:1 reference-guided multi-part procedural CNC/lathe assemblies (`bpy`/`bmesh` revolutions, flanges, stepped bores, bosses, webs, ribs, slots and grooves). Target genuine multi-part functional detail (screening floor `>= 25,000` visible evaluated triangles scene-wide; typical hero mechanical assemblies span `50k–250k+` evaluated triangles). Triangle counts and audit flags are technical screening diagnostics only—never subdivide empty cubes to inflate counts or treat counts as visual acceptance.
2. **Machined chamfers, clean normals, real wall thickness and assembly shadow gaps.**
   - **No razor-sharp 90° mathematical edges:** Every machined hero edge receives physical chamfers or an angle-limited `Bevel` modifier (`limit_method='ANGLE'`, ~30°, `segments >= 3`, `0.15–0.8 mm` machined micro-chamfers on CNC metal; `1–3 mm` fillets on cast/molded housings) so Cycles catches continuous crisp specular edge lines.
   - **Zero pillow shading or faceted curves:** Use smooth shading paired with `WeightedNormal` (keep sharp) or angle-controlled sharp edges / `EdgeSplit` so milled flats remain optically planar while fillets and cylinders shade cleanly. Never leave curved hero metal flat-shaded (`use_smooth=False`) or capped cylinders smooth-shaded without weighted normals or holding edges.
   - **Real wall thickness and physical seam gaps:** Every housing, cutaway wall, duct, bracket and transparent vessel requires actual dual-wall geometry or `Solidify` with beveled cut rims—never single-sided paper faces. Separate mating panels, covers and bolted flanges with crisp `0.15–0.5 mm` physical shadow/clearance gaps so contact occlusion and bevel speculars read as assembled industrial hardware.
   - **Applied uniform scale and outward normals:** Apply object scale to `(1.0, 1.0, 1.0)` and verify consistent outward normals before bevels, booleans, procedural directional grain or physics baking so bevel widths, anisotropic brushing and linked mechanical joints never skew.
3. **Functional industrial hardware vocabulary and strict neural-3D separation.**
   - Populate visible mechanical joints with real, engineering-accurate hardware where it belongs on the actual device: counterbored ISO hex-socket cap screws (ISO 4762 / DIN 912) with washers, stepped shoulders, retaining circlips, O-ring grooves, threaded shafts/nuts, real 3D diamond/straight knurling, cooling fins, perforated grilles with wall depth, bushings/bearings, stiffening ribs, and crimped hose/cable fittings entering coaxial ports. Avoid unmotivated random sci-fi greebles.
   - **Role-separated neural 3D (`TRELLIS.2` / `Hunyuan3D 2.1`):** Restrict single-image neural 3D meshes (`TRELLIS.2 --geometry-only` MIT shaded in Blender, or `Hunyuan3D 2.1`) strictly to **organic, crinkled, soft, porous or matte sculpted props** (food, crinkled foil packaging, fabrics, ceramics, organic forms). Never use marching-cubes neural SDF meshes for glossy CNC machined metals, optical glass, precision gears or tight-tolerance housings where surface ripples break specular highlights.
4. **Industrial PBR finish separation, studio lighting and clean optical physics.**
   - Separate adjacent components with distinct substrate + machining finishes under the same light: directional CNC brushed/turned aluminum (anisotropy aligned to machining axis), bead-blasted dark-anodized alloy, polished stainless/chrome shafts, subtle micro-textured sand-cast metal, satin powder-coated steel, molded engineering polymers (ABS/PEEK/nylon), and dry matte vulcanized rubber seals. Add subtle cavity AO darkening and edge-bevel roughness variation where physically motivated, avoiding uniform noisy grunge that muddies H.264 encodes.
   - Shape metallic form with broad studio softboxes / overhead linear light strips and dark negative-fill flags over grounded graphite/slate or clean matte industrial workbenches (preserving contact shadows and subtle floor reflection; no flat all-around wash, floating empty void, or emissive wireframe outlines).
   - Render glass, liquids and transparent housings with real wall thickness, beveled rims, physical refraction (`IOR`), continuous spatial thermal or cross-polarized photoelastic gradients where explained, and `visible_transmission = False` on bright area backlight strips so flat white glare bars never obscure internals. Size every explanatory force, stress, flow, or thermal cue for **360 px phone-screen readability** (`>= 1.5%–3%` of frame height—bold volumetric stress bands, unmistakable animated deformation, vivid translucent streamlines, or dramatic thermal/shockwave propagation; **never sub-pixel `<= 1 mm` micro-needles that disappear at viewing distance**), give cutaway cross-section faces a clean distinct solid cap finish, and obey the [3D single-letter rule](spatial-typography.md).
5. **Deterministic Colab Cycles baking, automated preflight and 1080p hero check.**
   - Drive collisions, shattering, tearing, cloth and fluid beats with real Blender simulations (`Bullet` rigid bodies, cloth, fluid/particle physics), then bake evaluated transforms into headless-safe **quaternion F-curves** (`evaluated_depsgraph_get()` -> `location` + `rotation_quaternion` keyframes, then remove `rigidbody_world`) so strided Colab multi-shard renders (`--shards N`) match 100% deterministically at arbitrary frame jumps. Pack all external images (`bpy.ops.file.pack_all()`) and verify zero missing linked libraries.
   - **Camera-path framing integrity and multi-shot editorial staging:** Verify every camera transition and orbit extreme against hero bounding boxes and solid shell geometry so cameras never pass through opaque doors/walls (black or stray interior flashes) or drift off-subject onto empty cloth/table backgrounds. Prefer **crisp multi-angle shot cuts** (`>= 2` purposeful framing/scale changes per 10 s: exterior action → clean cross-section → macro hero contact filling `>= 40%` of frame → side-by-side state comparison) over 30–40 s continuous single-shot camera orbits, and never leave a bare object idling in empty black void for 5+ seconds without active physical media, rays, forces, or state transitions.
   - Before uploading the packed `.blend` to Colab, run fast offline geometry/camera/packing preflight locally (`python -X utf8 scripts/colab_cli.py audit-blend --blend <scene.blend> --shards <N>`, zero local Cycles rendering). **Default ALL Blender Cycles rendering—including full-resolution `1920×1080` hero lookdev stills (`scripts/colab_cli.py blender-stills`), hook samples, and full sequences (`scripts/colab_cli.py blender-render`)—strictly to Google Colab GPU**; never run Cycles renders locally unless Colab is unavailable/unauthenticated or the user explicitly requests local-only rendering. Inspect the Colab-rendered `1920×1080` hero stills across every beat before expanding to the full sequence; `colab_cli.py` defaults to `--adaptive-threshold 0.015` and OpenImageDenoise `RGB_ALBEDO_NORMAL` guiding passes to preserve machined bevels, knurling and brushed grain, and records `industrial_modeling_audit` diagnostics in the receipt.

## Prevent High-Poly Emptiness: Multi-Stage Visual Density and Shot Choreography

High triangle counts, ISO bolts, and machined bevels on a **single isolated dark workbench** with one Bezier camera slowly drifting left and right for 25–40 s produce **"high-poly emptiness"**: the opening hook feels alive, then the entire body from second 5 onward looks sterile, repetitive, and hollow. For every complete `>= 20 s` 3D science episode, enforce four visual-density rules before rendering:

1. **Multi-world staging (never park `> 10–12 s` on one unchanged workbench):** Design at least **3 genuinely distinct visual arenas or scale worlds** across a 40–55 s episode (for example: real-world application environment → authentic full-context industrial/physical process with atmosphere and spatial depth → extreme macro cross-section / internal stress or fluid chamber → side-by-side split-screen A-vs-B controlled comparison). Sliding a camera 20 cm left/right in front of the same static bench prop does NOT count as a new visual beat.
2. **Discrete hard-cut camera grammar (no 30 s continuous Bezier glides):** Switch viewpoints cleanly between beats using stepped `CONSTANT` camera keyframes or multi-camera timeline markers (`>= 2` purposeful scale/angle shifts per 10 s target; `audit-blend` flags `hollow_single_stage_glide` on `>= 25 s` timelines with `< 3` hard cuts or `> 14 s` unbroken single glides). Avoid continuous interpolated fly-throughs between rooms or stages that clip through solid walls or doors.
3. **Mandatory spoken-verb visual fulfillment at phone scale:** Every concrete physical verb spoken by the narrator (heating, air-blast quenching, inward shrinking, elastic flexing, rebounding, latching, breaching, shockwave branching) must visibly dominate the center of a 360 px phone frame **during those exact spoken seconds**—never an invisible 1% flex, sub-pixel hairline cue, or 0.5 s off-beat blink while an idle slab sits on screen.
4. **Mandatory comparative or scale-jump spectacle inside the 5–35 s body:** Build at least one high-density visual anchor into the body—such as a **50/50 split-screen controlled comparison** (e.g., ordinary vs. tempered glass, blunt center impact vs. corner tap, down-stroke vs. up-stroke), an **extreme internal cross-section plunge** filling the frame with unmistakable layered forces/flows, or a dramatic real-world scale contrast—so viewers receive fresh visual discovery every 4–7 s.

## Cinema-Grade Blender Visual Richness: Replicating Live-Action Magnetism in Cycles

Everything that makes top live-action or macro cinema visually captivating—warm sunlit realism, fiery industrial glow, crisp bokeh depth, visceral slow-motion physics, and rich environmental storytelling—can be authored natively inside **Blender + Cycles** with 100% deterministic physical and geometric control. When a Blender film feels flat or hollow, fix these six Cycles cinematography levers rather than abandoning 3D:

1. **Per-beat lighting & color-script shifts (never one dim studio World for 50 s):** Assign each visual stage its own dedicated lighting collection / Light Linking / HDRI mood so visual energy evolves with the story—e.g., bright directional daylight exterior (`0–4 s`) → blazing incandescent furnace orange vs. icy cyan cold-air blast (`4–12 s`) → dramatic backlit optical macro chamber (`12–20 s`) → crisp dual-lit split-screen comparison → high-contrast rim-lit slow-motion climax.
2. **Edge-to-edge macro frame fill (`60%–90%` active subject coverage):** Never shoot a 50 cm specimen from 1.2 m away on a wide lens so it occupies `< 20%` of the frame inside an `80%` empty dark wall. On internal cross-sections and mechanism peaks, push macro/telephoto lenses (`65–105 mm`) right into the active physical layers so the working glass, fluid meniscus, stress zones, or latching teeth fill `60%–90%` of the mobile viewport.
3. **Real Cycles optical cinematography (shallow DOF, bokeh, `FOG_GLOW` & bounded volumetrics):** Replace flat `f/4.5–f/5.6` deep focus on bare back walls with purposeful shallow depth-of-field (`f/1.8–f/2.8`), rich foreground framing cues, and warm/cool background bokeh highlights. Use subtle Blender Compositor `Glare (FOG_GLOW)` for genuine glowing heat, sparks, or optical caustics, and bounded `Principled Volume` / procedural shader mist, steam, or turbulent air-blast streams wherever physically real.
4. **Modular per-shot cameras, collections & native 50/50 split-screens:** Do not cram an entire episode onto one shared light rig and one Bezier camera. Use named per-shot Cameras bound to Timeline Markers (or modular per-shot `.blend` files referencing one shared high-poly asset library) with instant collection/light-rig switches per cut, and author native **50/50 split-screen A-vs-B comparisons** (dual side-by-side setups or Compositor split) directly in Blender whenever two physical outcomes are contrasted.
5. **Tactile real-world environmental density:** Ground exterior and process shots in lived-in, believable environments (vehicles with real metallic paint & glass reflections, kitchen/workshop props, furnace ceramic arches & radiant coils, condensation, fabric weave, realistic contact shadows) rather than bare floating slabs over a flat dark plane.
6. **Unmistakable slow-motion physics & visual-verbal lock:** Choreograph real simulations (Voronoi fracture + Bullet rigid bodies, elastic bending, fluid flow, thermal shrinkage, shockwave propagation) at dramatic phone-readable scale and speed right on the exact spoken verb—never hiding the key deformation inside a `1%` flex or an off-beat `0.5 s` blink.

## Actual execution of bright lighting and materials

First define color roles and foreground/background duties under [professional color and spatial direction](art-direction.md#color-composition-and-space-design-the-viewing-order-first). Achieve the [bright-style standard](visual-system.md#bright-style-and-premium-materials) and applicable [industrial studio target](visual-system.md#industrial-product-studio-material-standard) in the actual camera, closest view and final engine. Use existing scene parameters, editable project and art review; do not add another project-wide experiment round.

For 3D material decisions, use the [general material standard](material-rendering.md) alongside the selected style. Record substrate, exposed finish and condition in the existing scene/material design; category names alone do not specify a shader. Before batch rendering, complete or validly reuse its grouped still/motion evidence through the producer's existing queue. This does not add an audio requirement, standalone approval or repeated test per duplicate part. Follow [appearance transfer into animation](material-rendering.md#carry-the-material-setup-into-the-actual-animation): develop the intended look alongside motion blocking and reuse the actual shader/light setup in the final rendering path, rather than treating a PBR asset still or viewport colors as the animated result.

1. **Check form and surface first.** Check real scale, applied scaling, normals, seams, necessary thickness and process-appropriate edge transitions. Explanatory silhouettes/interfaces require real geometry; maps cannot repair intersections, faceting or floating. Identify bare substrate, oxide/coating, polished or rough visible surfaces before setting each PBR response. Close-up perforations, sheets/layers and seams contributing silhouette, occlusion or parallax require real, correctly scaled wall thickness, layer spacing and cut edges, not hole dots or drawn seams; check closest-view readability. Do not invent nonexistent structure for detail; repeated parts may be instanced.
2. **Give objects light to reflect.** For industrial products, prioritize broad soft sources; tune source/reflector size, direction and position for the actual camera so metal curves show continuous bright bands and transitions through midtones into deep shadows. Fill only enough to read necessary interfaces. Real bevels and controlled reflection shapes produce fine clear thin-edge highlights, not thick white strips on every edge or emissive outlines. Use restrained edge light when separation needs it. Evaluate reflection width separately from boundary sharpness: a large panel may produce a broad yet crisp reflection on polished metal, while roughness softens its boundary. Unmotivated wobble across a nominally flat panel is a form/normal/finish defect, not added realism. Choose background value for composition: darkness does not require underlighting the subject. Adjust subject key/fill, reflected-source placement and flags first, preserving background/black-material depth rather than raising background, World and exposure together for “brightness.” Fill restores necessary structure; flags/negative fill may remove stray reflection without flattening. A rim with an unreadable front fails too. Adjust light direction, shape and size, not a fixed light count/power. If a single World is too flat or broad black reflections erase required form, surface distinctions or interfaces, repair that lighting/reflection gap; retain useful dark reflections that preserve observation. Only use source-compliant environmental inputs, not third-party background images. See the [Blender light manual](https://docs.blender.org/UATEST/manual/en/4.5/render/lights/light_object.html) for area shapes and soft shadows.
3. **Author materials separately.** These are fact-dependent starting points, not a per-shot material quota; check nodes/layers against the current engine version.

| Actual visible surface | Differences to show |
| --- | --- |
| Polished or semi-gloss metal | Use actual metallic response. Bright and dark surroundings remain parts of one continuous reflection field; sharper boundaries correspond to a more polished finish, without forcing every metal into a mirror |
| Blasted, matte or brushed metal | Rough metal has broader, blurred specular reflections; brushing follows the part's machining direction. Show overall directional sheen at distance and individual marks only at an appropriate close scale, without random grain |
| Paint, powder coating | Show coating color plus its actual gloss/satin/matte response. Gloss paint can reflect a recognizable panel/window; satin/matte blur it. Opaque paint is not bare metal; expose substrate only at supported coating boundaries, wear or cut sections. Add clear protective coat only when present |
| Engineering plastic, rubber | Distinguish glossy molded plastic, textured plastic and dry rubber under the same light, including black surfaces. Gloss plastic may carry clear environment reflections; fine-textured dry rubber generally has broader, weaker highlights. Preserve form/grazing response rather than imposing one weak-reflection preset or turning surfaces into chalk |
| Anodized/oxidized surfaces, ceramics and other materials | Follow each actual surface/process state; do not treat all oxidized metal as paint or give matte and glazed ceramics identical sheen |
| Glass, liquids, foam | Check actual thickness, transmission/refraction, appropriate IOR, roughness, absorption and animated surface flow/ripples plus visible meniscus contact at pipe walls and ports; reflections, front/back surfaces and objects seen through the material move consistently with their respective positions. Never render flowing liquid as an opaque smooth plastic rod or foam/splashes as opaque matte spheres stuck together; render multi-scale translucent glossy bubble clusters with Fresnel rim highlights when foam is real |
| Invisible gases, airflows and vacuum/pressure states | Keep gas/airflow visually unmistakable from both liquid and solid hardware: use continuous translucent wispy streamlines, directional flow ribbons or fine vapor streaks—never isolated solid spheres rolling along a metal rod—and show pressure/suction changes through physically coupled deformation (such as a flexing rubber diaphragm) rather than a flat glowing neon box alone |
| Wood, fabric, stone and textured surfaces | Grain/weave follows credible component orientation and cloth folds, with scale appropriate to distance. Distinguish varnished/unfinished or other actual finishes independently of pattern. Wear follows condition, handling, friction and exposure rather than filling every surface with scratches |
| Carbon-fiber and other composites | Match fiber direction, weave/texture scale and actual structure; surface resin follows actual finish state. Preserve black depth, revealing form through reflection and background separation |

For PBR layers, roughness, metal and transmission, see the [Blender Principled BSDF manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/sr/4.5/render/shader_nodes/shader/principled.html). Do not apply the same parameters to unlike materials or equate more samples with material design.

For material spill, dirty spots, seams or wrong scale, first inspect editable node scope and masks. Temporarily connect masks/single channels locally to emission unaffected by lighting to diagnose UV/object coordinates, frequency, values and blending instead of blindly adding noise/samples. Keep original nodes, restore real surface response after repair, and use diagnostic output only internally; it cannot replace material inspection under real light, motion and final encoding.

4. **Calibrate color through final output.** Record actual OCIO configuration, working/texture spaces, View Transform, Look, Exposure and encoding conversion. Distinguish color textures from roughness/normal/data maps. Linear EXR is not display-mapped PNG: avoid omitted/duplicate display transforms. In verified supported environments, applicable transforms such as AgX may protect highlights before target value calibration. Changing Look or globally raising exposure does not repair a film. Preserve scientific color meaning; check SDR/Rec.709 conversion and pixel ranges rather than just tagging color. See [Blender color management](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.4/render/color_management.html) for linear/display workflows.
5. **Deliver detail in motion.** Reuse existing representative shots at camera/subject rotation, darkest facing directions, strong reflection angles and transparent overlap. Under established light/reflection fields, highlights move smoothly along curves with viewpoint and surface orientation, continually explaining shape/material differences. Texture remains surface-attached; reflections must not stick to texture/screen coordinates or flicker/jump. Do not reset material, reflection field or exposure each frame to chase highlights. Review continuous footage for realism; stills cannot pass motion. Choose final engine/settings supporting required reflection, transmission and indirect light. Denoising must not erase fine lines/seams; sampling, filtering and encoding must not smear perforations, sheet/layer thickness and thin-edge highlights or create shimmer, breaks or plastic appearance. Compare masters/final encode to locate damage, then repair the relevant stage. Use local GPU, caches and batching for throughput without preemptively sacrificing materials/light.

## Blender backgrounds and spatial completion

The background is part of the shot's space, purpose and observation conditions and needs the subject's finished standard. Do not default an entire video to objects floating over the same solid/gradient, infinite platform or generic dark studio while only camera, background color and subtitles change. In existing scene cards choose required environment, reference basis, reusable assets and whole-to-local entry; “premium” is not a producible scene plan.

- **Establish specific context:** Application, whole-object and process views show topic-appropriate space, support/carrier, related objects and scale so viewers see location/use. Engineering settings may use evidenced workstations, factories, benches or installations; nature/life settings use actual carriers/observation conditions. Generic factories/arbitrary pipes cannot serve every machine. Do not decorate with unsupported connections, operations, cleanliness conditions or process states.
- **Organize readable depth:** Arrange subject, nearby referent and distant setting through distance, overlap, perspective and value, with purposeful foreground as needed. Check true parallax/continuity in movement. Do not force three layers into each frame or fake interactive/orbitable 3D space with one backdrop. Contact, support, shadows and reflections follow objects/environment; avoid floating, infinitely repeated tiles and empty stages.
- **Complete the environment:** At visible scale add necessary facility/architecture structure, seams, edges, material distinctions and suitable wear; adapt reused modules' placement, proportions and use. Key/environment light and reflections explain space while keeping subject clear and background quiet. HDRI, fog, depth of field, noise or colored light cannot replace content or conceal rough subject/background.
- **Bound simplification:** Mechanism close-ups, cutaways, microscopic diagrams and necessary comparisons may use restrained backgrounds but retain explanatory material boundaries, carriers or location cues and recognizable entry/return to the whole. Asset inspection, isolation and explicitly requested minimalism follow their use. Local simplicity does not permit an entire film to omit setting design; richness is not clutter or unrelated movement.
- **Follow content:** Design backgrounds/view scales for actual application → assembly/workstation → local mechanism → result needs. Reuse scenes without fixed counts. Process/scale changes alter genuinely relevant space/observation relationships, maintaining direction, lighting and machine identity within a setting. Arbitrary backgrounds cannot hide spatial breaks.
- **Review at existing gates:** Inspect full composite, necessary details and continuous normal-speed representative footage at target aspect. Whole-film thumbnails locate persistent same-background/composition and purposeless repeated settings, then inspect actual detail, occlusion, motion and captions/annotations. Missing context, placeholder backgrounds or known roughness in formal G3 require repair before expansion; G5 rechecks the current final encode. Flat-color area, object count or library inclusion cannot pass a background; background completion cannot offset subject/science failures.

Examples: Establish machine scale in an evidenced installation/test setting before a location-preserving structural close-up. Connect manufacturing stages through appropriate workstations and real input/output, then reduce periphery in local cuts. For microscopic material, establish macroscopic application/carrier, then explain in bounded material space. These are methods, not mandatory openings/facility lists.

## Character rigging, motion capture and lip sync

Enable this branch when a character's performance helps demonstrate an operation, interaction or explanation. Reuse a suitable local character, rig, actions and facial controls before building new ones. Choose the required output first: editable 3D performance belongs in Blender; generated face-video postprocessing produces a video layer. Follow the selected [production route](tool-routing.md#lock-the-primary-tool-by-final-film-style) and the actual [source/usage requirements](science-and-rights.md); an available tool or animation library does not expand the allowed asset origins or external uploads.

1. **Define the performance.** In the existing action card, name the character, target, gaze, action, contact/release events and resulting object response. Plan only the body, hands and facial detail visible at the closest shot. Reuse the same character identity and performance across camera cuts; a presenter need not speak or gesture continuously.
2. **Rig and skin separately.** Check mesh topology, scale, axes, transforms and rest pose. Fit joint positions, create or correct the skeleton, bind deformation weights, and test the actual bending/turning/grasping poses. Skeleton generation alone is not skinning. Correct collapsing shoulders/hips, elbow/knee twisting, disconnected clothing and hand/object penetration before rendering; preserve the editable source rig and mesh.
3. **Retarget and clean motion.** Map source/target bones, rest poses, units and root movement. Preserve the source fps, time origin and resampling map rather than silently treating every capture as 30 fps. Keep source motion separate from cleaned actions; correct foot sliding, ground contact, balance and held-object constraints. Body-motion recovery does not automatically supply reliable fingers, face or device operation. Bake the required actions/constraints for the actual batch-render path and test a different frame order when random-access evaluation is used.
4. **Animate speech from the approved audio.** Reuse the current [voice track](voice-reference.md), then map audio-relative mouth cues to facial bones/visemes or relative shape keys at the project fps and offset. Use a language-compatible aligner or manually corrected cues; Chinese characters/subtitle timings are not one-to-one mouth shapes. Keep curves editable, blend neighboring mouth shapes, preserve pauses/closed lips, and combine jaw, lips, expression and gaze without double deformation. Audio or edit changes invalidate the affected alignment, not automatically the entire rig.
5. **Test the real difficulty.** Use the existing G2/G3 sample to cover the planned close-up and difficult turn, occlusion, grasp/release or speech transition. Check deformation/contact in motion, watch normal-speed with the actual audio for synchronization, and inspect mouth/teeth/tongue clipping, identity, edge stability and silent sections. An idle pose, a walk cycle or one frontal talking still cannot validate these harder uses. Record actual observations and unreviewed ranges in the existing QA; tool completion is not performance approval.

## Shared geometry and orientation baseline

- Preserve one geometry source for physical parts reused across assembly, extraction, cutaway and ending views; reference the same asset/build functions rather than independently rebuilding lookalike substitutes.
- State applicable local axes, interfaces, motion axes and viewpoints before modeling/animation. For aerodynamic blades also check upstream/downstream, leading/trailing edges, root/tip and concave/convex faces; use sections/chords as needed for camber, installation and airflow, then verify from final camera. Do not impose blade terms on other mechanisms.
- **Installation rotation cannot repair mirrored airfoil camber.** On reversed-orientation feedback distinguish handedness, incoming flow, assembly pose, rotation direction and camera; identify the cause, change shared geometry/affected shots, and do not merely rotate a close-up or reverse arrows.
- Same-kind/stage repeats may share geometry. Adjacent rotors, guide vanes and different stages require their own function, rotation and speed checks; do not copy one airfoil/angle across the engine. Concave orientation, rotation and camber are not universal across models.
- For coaxial rotating blade rows, project evaluated meshes along the actual axis and use conservative axial envelopes to check adjacent separation throughout rotation. Include real root locks, disk rims, endwalls and bevel extremes, not just nominal chords or one angle. Positive gap supports only teaching geometry, not manufacturer operating, thermal or manufacturing tolerance.
- For close-up mechanisms, cutaways and flexible straps/ribbons/cables, pair mesh-interpenetration checks (such as `BVHTree.overlap` on visible evaluated meshes across representative rest, mid-travel and locked/actuated poses) with **bounded mating-proximity checks** (`0 < clearance <= max_contact_gap`) and **ribbon frame continuity checks**. Never satisfy `overlap == 0` by pulling connected parts apart into empty air: spring hook eyes wrap concentrically around anchor pins, latched connectors/tongues seat inside slotted receptacles, cam/ball/lever followers maintain visible tangential contact along open cutaway ramps and intermediate linkages, pivoted pedals/arms keep uniform parent scale (`(1, 1, 1)`) so non-uniform scaling never crushes child linkages into slivers, and wound rolls/spools rotate coherently with attached flanges and peeling straps. Route flexible ribbons/straps in consistent world/parent coordinates (keeping position transforms distinct from normalized direction vectors) through every intermediate guide and latch without 180-degree normal flips or floating loops that bypass anchors, and inspect full-resolution preflight stills of both close-up mechanism interfaces and whole-carrier routing before batch rendering.
- Illustrative orange/blue denotes only explained variables; streamlines still require real coordinate support. Distinguish stationary-frame absolute flow and blade-frame relative flow; do not join them without transformation as one “validated” path. Curves, particles and colors are not CFD/measurement evidence.

## Move continuously from the whole into the local view

- Locate the target within the whole before moving closer. When exposing internals, choose a local cutaway, shell removal or selective transparency that reveals the needed region while retaining enough silhouette, adjacent structure and interfaces to recognize its original position. Keep relevant wall thickness and cut edges readable. Preserve recognizable features across shots; a matching name, hard cut or dissolve alone does not establish component identity.
- Start bridge actions from assembled-scene world transforms evaluated at the actual start frame; align the target close-up ending pose, camera and light. Account for parents, evaluation, units and scale to prevent flipping, jumping or replacement during extraction.
- First clear occlusion and adjacent geometry in the actual scene, then rotate for inspection. Check start, closest obstacle, rotation extremes, joins and intervening movement. Teaching separation/cutting/extraction is not a real maintenance/disassembly procedure.
- Check obstruction roles for local inspection. Handle supports/fasteners dependent on the removed shell together in applicable display groups; do not leave thick unattached rods obscuring the subject. Save inspection states in named views/layers, preserving default complete assets; do not remove necessary supports indiscriminately for cleanliness.
- Shell, internals and holes share a shape definition. After shell removal its hole rows, trailing exits and silhouette still correspond; do not hide wrong connections with plates or geometric breaks with cuts.

### Choose exploded views or cutaways

Use an exploded view to explain component order, interfaces and assembly relationships; use a cutaway to reveal a path or mechanism that must remain connected. Select the view for the observation task, rather than cutting and rejoining an object repeatedly to create motion.

For a moving cutaway, design the complete opening/hold/closing cycle with the final camera path. A finite Boolean box is not an infinite plane: verify its intended cutting face and all other faces across the entire swept volume, including thin end plates, rollers and caps. Derive camera clearance and visibility from the final evaluated scene state, not a cached base camera or an isolated endpoint. Keep the physical liquid/material state separate from the observation cut so the reveal does not imply draining or removal. When a cutaway exposes a multi-stage nested mechanism (four or more linked parts such as sensing ports, diaphragms, ball latches, sleeves and valves), apply progressive visual isolation: dim, desaturate or temporarily ghost non-active concentric housings and walls during each causal beat so the active moving interface fills a phone-readable scale instead of disappearing inside busy red/metallic shells. If one patch creates another cut-face/occlusion defect, validate the shared geometry/state representation through the [complete repair cycle](blender-execution.md#complete-a-repair-cycle-before-expanding) before adding more local offsets or wrapper overrides.

- Start from the recognizable assembled object and keep a fixed reference part. In the existing action card, name the component groups, assembly axis/axes, separation vectors, reveal order and interfaces the viewer must see. A layered stack usually reads clearly along one principal axis; choose other paths when its actual topology requires them.
- Separate each group once along an intentional path, preserving orientation during that move. A translation-only plan is `M_i(t) = T(e(t) * d_i) * M_i(0)`, with initial evaluated world transform `M_i(0)`, planned world-space offset `d_i` and smooth non-overshooting progress `e(t)`. Convert to the actual parent space; avoid applying the offset to both a parent and its children. Rotation, when needed for inspection, is a separate readable phase.
- Stage **assembled recognition → separation → interface observation → reassembly or matched handoff**. Reassembly is useful only when the next explanation needs it. Space parts enough to expose their connections at phone size; preserve necessary fasteners, supports and connector identity. Keep camera movement quiet while the layers become distinguishable.
- Check start, intermediate, fully separated and handoff states, then normal-speed playback: the same parts remain recognizable, paths do not intersect accidentally, and the exposed interfaces answer the question. Explain the transition from a structural tour to a manufacturing sequence through the narration/edit; display order does not establish real production or servicing order.

## Visible connections in cooling and piping mechanisms

- Derive channel, partition, inlet/outlet, passage and surface-film positions/directions from shared geometry/surface functions, updating together after airfoil changes. Do not move shells while leaving internal paths/flow lines in old coordinates.
- Check that inlets enter cavities, bends remain open, partitions do not inadvertently penetrate and exits are actual holes. Lines on metal, touching cylinders or glowing points cannot replace fluid passages.
- Film flow starts at existing holes, following the corresponding blade surface downstream in its coordinate frame; do not pass coolant through solids or reverse explanation direction for beauty. Mesh/visible-frame checks have distinct scopes; lack of obvious penetration cannot establish hidden topology or flow validation.

## Bounded trials and reliable reuse

### Qualify critical assets before whole-film integration

For a main subject, close-up part or shared asset whose failure would invalidate several shots, establish its usable shape, necessary structure and deformation at the closest intended view before adapting it across the film. Use representative views and relevant rest/extreme states in the existing grouped samples; reuse evidence that covers this use. A successful import, intact texture binding or numerical rig test does not approve the asset's appearance. This is dependency ordering within G2/G3, not an extra approval or a test for every background prop.

Keep a stable asset source and only the interfaces needed next: units, axes, root/pivots, deformation controls and contact/outlet anchors. Interface sketches, local deformation tests and low-cost story/motion blocking may proceed with explicit proxies. If the asset is visibly failed or its key structure remains unresolved, stop detailed whole-film adapters, repeated per-shot compatibility scans and dependent beauty renders. Correct the shared construction or select a suitable licensed replacement before that work resumes; texture polish cannot repair silhouette or topology. Then validate one complete high-risk interaction and its joins before expansion. Qualification supports only its observed asset/use scope; the actual scene and complete film still need their applicable reviews.

Use the existing G2/G3 workflow in three production passes; these are work products, not extra approvals:

| Pass | Produce and check | Keep costly work out until |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous motion preview | Use existing assets or adequate placeholders at normal playback fps. Include the opening context, transition and first topic observation; check camera path, object identity, occlusion, attention and timing with the available narration. Lower spatial/shading cost, not temporal sampling. For a feed film's [hook shot](viral-appeal-and-packaging.md#win-the-first-two-seconds), start the action and camera already in motion at the film's first frame (pre-roll the simulation or offset the action; no ease-in from rest on shot 1) and frame the subject large enough to read inside a phone-feed band; the hook shot is the first to receive final lighting and materials. | The route and adjacent-shot handoff work in continuous playback. Do not wait for polished geometry to discover a weak transition. |
| Final-engine look development | Check representative final-resolution frames and a short continuous risk segment for structure, reflections, transparency, cut edges, fibers and temporal noise. Use the approved final engine and closest planned viewing scale. | The actual material/geometry risks are covered. Preview-engine success alone does not establish final appearance. |
| Shot render and edit | Render only affected shots/ranges to original image sequences, then assemble with editable permitted single-letter 3D passes, separate ordinary synchronized 2D captions and the accepted voice track. Check the resulting joints and complete applicable audiovisual scope. | Existing G3 conditions are met before expansion; final quality still requires actual viewing/listening. |

Keep one reusable asset source and a stable shot driver; store shot-specific camera, animation and frame parameters in the project. Build/import scenes once per process and update their states per frame. Repeated parts can share meshes/instances; retain required structure at the actual closest shot. Do not recreate whole assets or launch scripts for every label, camera or duration revision.

Use the [global sequence executor](blender-execution.md) for suitable local intermediate frames: immutable input jobs, native frame ranges, exact-device selection, per-frame hashes, resumable output and separate build/render timing. Reuse an existing compatible executor where it already implements these behaviors. Existing task-specific safeguards remain applicable. A saved procedural scene must be baked or supplied with its actual state driver before background/farm rendering.

Determine rerender scope from changed dependencies: common geometry/material changes affect referencing shots; camera changes affect the shot and its junctions. A 2D subtitle/audio correction can usually reuse verified picture when timing matches. Only necessary single-letter markers are allowed in 3D picture, editable in scene geometry or a matching 3D letter pass under the [single-letter rule](spatial-typography.md); explanatory words, values/units and plaques must be removed, not restyled; a change rerenders that pass and affected shadows/reflections/occluders, not automatically the whole scene and not merely a replacement 2D overlay. Validate reused raw frames against original scene/input/settings and frame hashes. Export actual anchor projections/visibility where useful for [dynamic annotations](annotation-style.md#motion-and-object-binding); metadata alone does not prove visibility, spatial depth or readability.

Freeze code, assets, engine/settings and frame mapping before a batch. A changed source creates a new version; a retry with identical inputs verifies and resumes the original job. Keep historical outputs and failed attempts. Review unchanged evidence within its original scope instead of redoing it for bookkeeping; use the [context comparison](production-checkpoints.md#record-normalization-and-incremental-review) for triage. Newly exposed geometry, changed meaning, known failures and changed audiovisual joins still need the affected checks.

Reuse the accepted continuous narration; place necessary observation pauses at semantic breaks and update picture, captions and annotations together. Measure preparation, scene build, render/write, edit and review separately. Technical frame/geometry checks do not certify actual appearance, science or hearing, and an optimization is not a measured speedup until comparable work has been timed.

### Open-licensed models may be adopted directly

Search local/shared assets first, then target open-model libraries for the specific missing object. Matching use-compatible licensed models may be downloaded with necessary files and linked/appended/imported directly, without per-model reapproval or rebuilding for “originality.” Modify only needed materials, scale, structure, rig or animation; retain usable parts. Download only current models/dependencies; prefer caches to whole-library or repeat transfers.

Check specific model page, creator and actual license for current publication/commercial use, modification, attribution and bundled textures/materials. “Free,” downloadable or open-source repository code does not establish model rights. Keep versions/hashes, license evidence and real modifications. Fulfill actual attribution in appropriate descriptions/companions; incompatible delivery/picture-text terms require another model, not an unauthorized plaque.

Open the model and inspect identity, units/axes, proportion, topology, internals, close-up detail, materials and required motion/cutaway. Availability does not establish science or suitability: fix actual gaps, retain suitable parts. Import data without auto-running package scripts. Record external authorship and actual project staging/animation/rendering separately, not self-created model claims. Model prerenders/promotional stills are not permitted production imagery by this route. See [licensed-model provenance](science-and-rights.md#licensed-3d-model-inputs).

Deduplicate identical catalog files by SHA-256, preserving sources, original versions and review scopes. `reusable` is a starting point only within listed scope, not new-film audiovisual/scientific/publication approval. `pending` lacks sufficient review/dependency evidence; `failed` records a failed version/diagnostic counterexample with cause/scope. Preserve conflicting context results as pending. Verify target files/dependencies before use; index presence is not availability.

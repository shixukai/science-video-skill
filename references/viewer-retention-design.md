# Sustained viewing: organize explanation as rewarding discovery

Own whole-film narrative flow, newcomer orientation, opening-to-body bridges, section progression, and competing-attention diagnosis — reusing `opening`, `payoff_steps`, action cards, and stage QA without extra forms.

## Direct the whole film before polishing its parts

The parent owns one coherent story across five levels before delegating shots: **Overall context** (why care) → **This episode** (concrete question & post-watch takeaway) → **Case/demonstration** (why this object/example) → **Development** (what each beat adds & why the next follows) → **Resolution** (how the earned answer reframes the opening).

Read the complete G1 script without leaning on titles or task chat; fix any promise/body/ending disconnect BEFORE full narration synthesis or batch refinement. No fixed runtime, chapter count, or word count — let clear explanation set length, keep spatial worlds sustained across consecutive shots (never ping-pong exterior/studio single shots), and place boundary/edge cases inside the beat whose variable they test. G2/G5 verify what the finished media actually conveys.

### Frame knowledge as problem-solving or story, never a lecture

Pure fact delivery feels like a classroom lecture and triggers drop-off even when every claim is accurate. Embed the mechanism inside a concrete scenario so viewers follow a causal plot rather than memorizing isolated principles:
- **Everyday person/engineering problem (`everyday_problem_solving`):** Open inside a concrete daily snag or design bottleneck (a driver pumping gas, a cook frying eggs, an engineer preventing an elevator fall) and reveal each structural piece only when it solves the next constraint.
- **Verified historical incident (`verified_historical_incident`):** Use a primary-source-verified real failure, accident, or engineering puzzle that forced the discovery — enter **at the moment of physical conflict**, never slow chronological biography (`它有着悠久的历史……`), and never present AI-staged reconstructions as real archival documentary evidence.
- **Hypothetical "What-If" boundary test (`what_if_counterfactual`):** Advance the mechanism by asking and visibly testing concrete counterfactuals (`如果把枪口旁边这根小孔堵住，油还会继续往外喷吗？` / `如果把客机窗户改成直角，机身会先从哪儿裂开？`), letting each visible outcome earn the next step.
- **Show-first visual replacement:** Before writing explanatory prose for any abstract quantity (force, pressure, heat, flow, field, scale), pair it with a concrete animation, cutaway, or side-by-side experiment so the picture proves the point while voiceover stays minimal causal glue.

## Explain like a human: two-part mental model and strict referent lock

Technical precision means nothing if an ordinary viewer watching at `1.0×` speed cannot tell **what stays fixed, what moves, and which object on screen the narrator is talking about**. Design every explanation from the eyes and working memory of a cold first-time human viewer rather than an engineer reciting a CAD parts list:

1. **Establish a two-part mental model before opening any complex cutaway.** Before revealing 4+ nested internal parts, anchor the mechanism in **at most two plain-language physical actors** and **one everyday baseline state**:
   - **Lock the frame of reference first (fixed vs. moving):** Show and state plainly *which part stays anchored to the floor/wall/body* and *which part actually moves* before explaining forces or pressures acting between them (for example, in a gas-lift office chair: *the center rod and piston stay bolted to the chair base, while the outer gas cylinder rides up and down with your seat*). Never launch into pressure math on an interior surface while the viewer still thinks the stationary part is what moves.
   - **Teach the intuitive baseline before the counter-intuitive twist:** Demonstrate the simplest closed/normal state with an everyday physical anchor (for example, *valve shut = a sealed bicycle pump cushioning your weight*; *standard mixer = two inlet doors feeding one chamber*) before introducing the twist (opening a bypass path, unequal opposing areas, or a thermal actuator sliding a gate).
   - **Zero jargon dumps ahead of visible action:** Never stack 2+ unfamiliar mechanism nouns (`slide valve`, `rubber sleeve`, `valve pin`, `concentric bypass wall`) before the viewer has seen each part isolated and acting on screen. Call a part by its plain physical role first (`the sliding brass sleeve`, `the small pin at the top`) and drop specialist jargon completely when plain speech is clearer.
2. **Enforce strict sentence-to-screen referent lock (ban "self-talk" narration).** Every spoken sentence (`~2–4 s`) must point to **one unmistakable, visually active referent on screen at that exact timestamp**:
   - **1-Second Layperson Pointing Test:** Cover the brief and read ONLY the spoken subtitle line paired with its rendered frame. A cold viewer must be able to put a finger on the exact named part, distinguish fixed from moving, and see the exact stated physical change within `< 1 s` without guessing.
   - **Match the physical subject, never a stand-in:** If narration says *"gas pushes upward on the full circular top cap of the cylinder and downward against the smaller ring around the rod at the bottom"*, the camera MUST show the **moving cylinder's actual top end-cap and bottom rod-pierced annulus** (or unfold both surfaces side by side)—never stare tightly at the stationary middle piston with faint decorative discs pasted onto it.
   - **No unintroduced visual teleports or mystery props:** Never cut abruptly from an interior cutaway to an isolated floating white cube, unmarked glowing block, or side-by-side floating test tubes without a visible spatial bridge showing what each prop is and where it came from.

| Human-comprehension failure ("self-talk") | Human-first repair |
| --- | --- |
| Narrator names `slide valve`, `rubber boot`, and `hot/cold ports` over a crowded all-brass cutaway where every metal ring looks identical | Dim/ghost the outer housing first, isolate ONE high-contrast moving sleeve between unmistakable crimson hot and cobalt cold ports, and show the gate physically narrowing one opening as it widens the other |
| Narrator explains unequal top/bottom gas-force areas while the camera stays zoomed on a stationary middle piston inside a narrow vertical tube | First establish *base + steel rod stay fixed; seat + outer cylinder move*; then pull wide enough to show the cylinder's real top cap vs. bottom rod-pierced ring—or unfold both circular faces horizontally side by side across the 16:9 frame |
| Script cuts straight from an everyday problem to a floating abstract white cube or floating unanchored comparison tubes with no visual introduction | Bridge continuously from the recognizable real object (e.g., shower handle/cartridge or chair lever/cylinder) into the exact internal element being compared, keeping recognizable context visible |

## Establish context and a reason to watch before explaining

Give newcomers a recognizable object and concrete interest before dependent terms or procedures, using [the opening workbench](viral-appeal-and-packaging.md#win-the-first-two-seconds) and [host-to-part bridge](viral-appeal-and-packaging.md#anchor-a-part-inside-its-host):
- **Recognize context** on moving Frame 0 (or recognizable working host bay within 0–5 s).
- **See a worthwhile question** grounded in a real visible difficulty, contrast, or stakes.
- **Lock the [concrete viewing promise](viral-appeal-and-packaging.md#lock-a-concrete-viewing-promise-by-about-8-seconds)** by ~8 s without reciting a table of contents.
- **Enter step 1 smoothly** with continuous object/scale anchors; move history and definitions to their point of use.

### Orient the viewer before introducing a detail

A viewer arriving **without the title, cover, description or task chat** must tell **what this is about, what is happening or being asked, and why the first detail matters** — establishing minimum shared context **before a component, procedure, unexplained number or technical term depends on it**. Name an unfamiliar object or function naturally when picture alone cannot; **Necessary naming is not a title echo** or throat-clearing.

Design first line and first readable shot together. **They need not use the same sentence order**, and the full answer need not appear upfront; simply ensure demonstratives ("this"/"these") have a visible referent and avoid fixing missing orientation with greetings, history, or title plates.

In G1 reads and G2/G5 cold views, hide external packaging, paraphrase subject + question from the opening alone, and flag the first unsupported referent. Distinguish subject, visible clue, and capture tool: **If the subject is the specimen or phenomenon**, open on it and make the clue legible; **If the subject is how the tool works**, connect tool, target, and result. **Leave the explanation open**; **do not demand spoken repetition** of what the picture already shows, and judge final openings on combined audiovisual media (**a script-only check cannot approve a planned image**). This is **not a new gate, fixed sentence template or extra timing quota**.

Calibration cases:

| Case | Opening and picture | Expected diagnosis / bounded repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detail without an object | Sharp electron-microscope texture or unlabeled dots; "The beam scans one row at a time." | Fails orientation: establish sample/result and real question before scan procedure; choose proper `host_relation`. |
| Instrument and question together | Instrument/sample context; "How does an electron microscope make a picture with electrons?" + first observation | Orientation pass: instrument, task, and question connect. |
| Same image, different subject | Crystal film: verified micrograph identifies sample & pattern. Microscopy film: links sample/pattern to instrument resolving it. | In the first film **the instrument can stay offscreen**; name what marks represent **only if the source identifies them**. |
| A recognizable everyday object | Visible zipper opening; "Why does this zip split behind the slider?" | Orientation pass without repeating formal definitions; image supplies referent. |
| Topic word without a connection | "Electron microscope." over anonymous dots + unexplained detector parameter | Still fails: naming alone doesn't link object, result, and question. |
| Background instead of orientation | "Today we will discuss a very important instrument, with a long history..." | Cut format/history; pair needed subject with concrete event/question. |

### Make opening pictures deliver the application context

When narrating everyday use, show recognizable real objects/working interfaces rather than dark blocks or generic textures; review continuously through the first body observation before expansion.

## Connect the opening to the first body observation

Write the opening's final line and the body's first line together under [spoken-script editing](voice-reference.md#edit-narration-and-dialogue-for-the-ear), pairing `final_state` → `initial_state` in `visual_handoff` / `handoff`:
- **Guard the 5–15 s post-hook dip:** Move directly from the ~8 s viewing promise into the first visible action, comparison, or micro-prediction test — never drop into a static multi-part structure overview (`主要由三部分组成`). Keep 0–8 s hook + promise inside one continuous TTS paragraph.
- **Visual-verbal counterpoint:** Let the picture carry the literal visible motion while narration adds hidden cause, stakes, or expectancy tension (never "narrate the pixels").
- **Whole-to-local:** locate target in whole, then enter close/sectional view keeping orientation.
- **Graphic/action match:** match silhouette or motion phase across the cut without implying false causation.
- **Product back to manufacturing:** signal time reversal explicitly after a result preview.
- **J/L-cut sound bridge:** overlap continuous ambience/speech smoothly across cuts without restarting narration mid-breath.

Use straight cuts when relationships already read clearly; follow the single commissioned route under [SKILL.md's Route Isolation gate](../SKILL.md#route-isolation-load-only-the-active-route).

### Engineering example: from application to chip etching

The hook lab must select an oriented opening and **not put anonymous fine lines first** while promising chip context later. The compact flow below is a **coherent alternative application-led route** (not mandatory preamble):
1. Phone shutter → photo on screen → locate chip: “随手按下快门，照片马上就出现在屏幕上。背后干活的这枚芯片，制造时要在极薄的材料里一层层雕出精细结构。”
2. Reveal difficulty on thin layer: “到了这一步，有些地方要刻掉，旁边的区域又必须完好留下来。真正讲究的地方，是只动指定的那一小块，不能波及周围。”
3. Narrow to mask + opening: “办法其实很巧：先盖上一层保护膜挡住要保留的地方，只把需要刻蚀的位置从开口处露出来。”
4. Immediate visible removal: “刻蚀一开始，露出来的材料就一点点往下凹进去，原本平整的薄层上，慢慢就显出了想要的图形。”

## Locate resistance to continued viewing first

Check normal-speed samples for sticking points: **opening with a textbook definition, formula, or lecture premise**, missing context before definitions, **"self-talk" audio-visual disconnect** (narration naming parts, surfaces, or force directions that the viewer cannot immediately point to in the frame, or staring at a stationary stand-in while describing a moving part), **missing fixed-vs-moving frame of reference before internal cutaways**, **unintroduced visual teleports or mystery props**, **three-way mismatch between title, feed header, and the first 10 s opening**, **prematurely spoiling the core answer inside the 0–8 s opening**, **5–15 s static structure-inventory sag or action-less/ray-less setup holds (`> 5 s` without visible physical state change)**, **uninterrupted lecture-density stretches (`> 20–30 s` without a visual reveal, mini-experiment, or curiosity-turning breathing point)**, **talk-over / PPT-text explanation** (relying on spoken prose or text overlays to explain an abstract concept that the frame fails to demonstrate visibly), **single-shot orbiting monotony** (holding one cutaway angle for 25–40 s instead of purposeful multi-scale cuts), **camera clip-through black frames or off-subject blank background drift**, **redundant pixel-narrating voiceover** (repeating what the image already shows without adding causal insight), **auditory working-memory overload** (`> 1` new entity or unanchored number in one spoken sentence), prolonged terminology before visible intuition, **flat additive ("and then" / `此外`/`另外`) transitions without causal or tension pull**, repeating an already-given answer, lost object identity across cuts, tiny unreadable changes ([animation scene design](animation-scene-design.md#make-existing-change-readable-as-evidence)), overloaded 4–6-part cutaways, **premature verbal ending signals** (`总结一下`/`所以说到底`/`in conclusion`) before the cut, or **post-answer life-tip/trivia tangents (`> 3.5 s` after resolving the main question)**.

## Why watch the next section after this one?

Link each section in `payoff_steps` (`contribution`, `opens_question`, `closes_question`, `time_s`) under [open-loop retention](viral-appeal-and-packaging.md#keep-one-question-open-until-the-final-answer) and [payoff cadence](viral-appeal-and-packaging.md#payoff-cadence-causal-pivots-and-re-hooks) using real discovery moves driven by **"Therefore / But" causal momentum** and a concrete **scenario/problem-solving arc** (never flat encyclopedia lists): follow the physical consequence (`Therefore`), hit an unexpected constraint or false summit (`But — solving step 1 creates a new hurdle`), test a concrete "what-if" condition change, converge parallel paths, return local → whole, show limits, or revisit the opening phenomenon. Control information density by closing each small knowledge block (every `~15–25 s`, never exceeding `30 s`) with a deliberate **breathing-point micro-climax** — a crisp visual transformation, a controlled mini-experiment/comparison, or a gentle curiosity-turning question paired with a brief foley breath — and always **show and narrate the visible physical behavior before introducing its technical term** so cognitive fluency stays effortless while the curiosity gap stays taut.

## Express relationships through shots; use montage as needed

Prefer continuity editing for orientation → local entry → key change → whole result; use purposeful montage ([AFI](https://www.afi.com/news/what-is-film-editing-and-what-does-a-film-editor-do/)) to compress repeated cycles (after teaching one complete cycle), compare parallel paths, or bridge scales — preserving qualifiers in narration/captions.

## Stage key discoveries in sound and picture

Sequence discovery cleanly: **establish target & reference → show readable change while conditions stay trackable → connect result to meaning**. For multi-stage internal mechanisms (4+ linked parts), **chunk into 2–3 isolated causal beats**, dimming/ghosting inactive housings so tiny active interfaces stay unmistakable at phone size, and fold secondary edge cases into the beat they test.

## Let the roughcut determine order and duration

Edit the voiced rough cut by comprehension value: delete/combine what loses zero prerequisites or evidence, move background to point of use, pull evidence earlier without spoiling, extend holds only after framing is clear, and compress already-learned cycles.

## Make the opening image understandable by the ending

Return to the opening subject/image with renewed understanding and finish promptly under [ending timing](viral-appeal-and-packaging.md#end-fast-after-explaining-the-mechanism) (`ending.answer_time_s`) — zero trailing questions, recaps, or new topics. Prefer a [loop-friendly last frame](viral-appeal-and-packaging.md#engineer-the-emotional-payoff-aha-frame-and-share-triggers) that echoes Frame 0's subject and composition so an autoplay replay feels continuous.

## Handoff to production agents and internal review

Pass exact version, defect interval, preserved conditions, and intended visible change using existing `opening`, `payoff_steps`, `explanation_steps`, `expression_cards`, and G2/G5 QA records. Reviewers watch/listen cold at 1.0× speed first and log the first breakdown before consulting author intent.

## Diagnose competing attention before making the edit faster

When a segment feels hard to follow, check what the viewer must locate, hear, read, and compare **simultaneously**. Fix the smallest conflict first (introduce referent before term, delay decorative camera moves, pair qualification beside comparison, separate narration/visual/mix issues) before speeding up edits or resynthesizing tracks.

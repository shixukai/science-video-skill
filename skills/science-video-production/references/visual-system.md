## Bright Style and Premium Materials

Realistic and Blender production default to effective lighting that makes the subject bright, clear, and finely finished while retaining real colors, necessary shadows, dark-area structure, and highlight detail. Choose background value independently; dark/black backgrounds are valid. Do not judge brightness by a pale backdrop or whole-frame average. Use [professional art direction](art-direction.md) to establish color roles, light/dark hierarchy, foreground/background, material reflections, and cross-shot light/color, and address snow-like noise by category. Reuse historical references within their original applicable scope; a dark background alone is not a style failure. Parameters or swatches do not equal approval of an actual sample. At G3/G5, check current actual media under existing art-failure records. For user review, provide clickable local file links under [delivery through material links](tool-routing.md#review-media-without-repeated-uploads).

### Industrial Product Studio Material Standard

Industrial products, equipment, and parts use a clean, realistic industrial product studio style. Use broad soft lighting for continuous curved-surface gradients, deep readable shadows and fine, distinct bevel/thin-edge highlights. Actual substrate and finish follow the [general 3D material standard](material-rendering.md): polished metal, satin paint, glossy molded plastic and dry textured rubber must not collapse into category-wide presets. Gloss paint and glossy plastic may show clear environmental reflections; matte finishes soften their boundaries. Close views reveal mesh, panel and seam thickness; moving highlights describe the same continuous form.

Studio style specifies lighting and surface-presentation quality, not a required pale background or removal of explanatory environment, interfaces and process actions. Preserve deep-shadow layers and necessary structure instead of flattening shadows, mirroring every surface or adding glowing outlines. Follow the [Blender implementation](engineering-blender.md#actual-execution-of-bright-lighting-and-materials) and the general standard's grouped still/motion tests; use qualifying existing sequences and extract the still from the same render when possible. Reuse this evidence within G3/G5 without a separate turntable approval or frame-upload sequence. Static assets establish only observed static appearance; actual continuous video establishes moving reflections.

### Visual Anchors of the Approved Style

The historical 2D reference uses **finely painted 2D nature illustration with credible structure, nuanced light and shadow, and clear, luminous material rendering**. View the [approved appearance reference](../assets/style/accepted-style-reference.png) and assign object, whole-scene, and mechanism tasks under the [art specification](../assets/style/bright-nature.art-direction.json). Retain the approved appearance direction; do not use the reference as footage. Ignore viewer backgrounds, sidebars, selection boxes, and screenshot cropping. Research scientific internal structures separately in reliable sources.

| Fixed style relationship | Implementation in a new topic | Explicit deviation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Representational form and structure | People have credible proportions, poses, hands, and feet; animals, plants, and artifacts have contours and structures based on their actual identities. Differentiate curves, changes of plane, and shapes under load | Reducing natural subjects to default icons, round blobs, polylines, or geometric placeholders whose identity depends entirely on labels |
| Organized volumetric light and shadow | Use 2D color masses, brushwork, and gradients to organize light, penumbra, and shadow along form transitions; local occlusion/contact strengthens structural credibility | The same flat fill over an entire object, arbitrary bright edges, or a uniform gradient; glow/global sharpening compensating for weak form |
| Distinct materials | Fabric folds follow joints and loading directions; equipment has parts and thickness differences. Transparent, metallic, geological, or biological surfaces show the features relevant to this topic | Applying the same highlights, paper texture, or rounded corners to every object; failing to distinguish soft, hard, transparent, and opaque surfaces |
| Hierarchy in edges and brushwork | Focal structures are clearer; secondary edges and distant views recede appropriately. Color masses and painted edges jointly define form | Every contour equally thick/hard, every region equally sharp or equally over-detailed; a crude subject embedded in a refined background |
| Natural light/color and atmospheric depth | Establish clear light direction, environmental reflected color, and near/far relationships for the scene. Warm light and cool air may provide depth; scientific colors take priority | Copying blue/green/yellow hex codes without value/spatial relationships; arbitrarily tinting night scenes, oceans, or mechanisms as the same sunlit scene |
| Equal finish in whole scenes, subjects, and mechanisms | The whole scene has spatial organization; the subject holds structure on close inspection; mechanism close-ups preserve object identity, material, and form finish. Diagram lines remain distinct from objects | A refined exterior abruptly becoming crude circles and thin-line teaching slides for the internal principle; decoration replacing key structure |

These relationships apply across topics. What stays fixed is production finish and painting language, not yellow clothing, climbers, valleys, leaves, or water droplets. Scientific graphics, coordinates, and paths may be simplified by function. A subject may be transparent, sectioned, or abstracted when needed, provided accurate correspondence and the same art finish are retained. Do not invent structure to create realism. Pure 2D permits painted volume and reflections, without introducing 3D models or pseudo-3D rendering.

### From Cover Finish to Controllable Dynamic Scenes

Carry the cover's subject, materials, light/color, and scene finish into the film, redesigning composition for each explanatory task. First select the representative segment most at risk of reducing a concrete object to a diagram, and complete the following within existing G2/G3 and action cards:

1. **Define a closed explanatory scope**: include whole-object orientation, key relationships, and a visible result. A causal topic may use “phenomenon → detail → mechanism → result”; comparisons or structural topics follow their own relationships. Isolated diaphragms, arrows, or beautiful exteriors do not cover this risk.
2. **Make relationships distinguishable first**: for each shot, state the subject, what is held/changed/compared, the result, and the connection back to the whole. Establish shot size and attention center at the target phone size; arrange backgrounds and text after necessary contacts, differences, and structures are clear.
3. **Prepare assets for the action**: produce required key states and complete hidden surfaces exposed by movement. Split subjects, backplates, and material layers by independent motion/occlusion, fixing coordinate roots, anchors, and masks. Backgrounds requiring no motion need not be split. Preserve identity, light direction, materials, and alpha across states.
4. **Make the objects fulfill the explanation**: create continuous states in the locked medium. Controllable implementations share relevant geometry and state parameters; inspect AI outputs item by item. Claims about quantity, strength, or repeatability require corresponding object results under a common scale/conditions. Diagrams must not pass as measurements. Panning/zooming a whole image, updating labels, or background particles cannot replace subject change.
5. **Preview with the existing narration**: arrange action, bridges, and observation pauses by actual meaning. View the complete composite, key states/motion extremes, and the normal-speed voiced segment. Verify that the result is distinguishable at phone size, then record issues and repairs under [stage evidence](quality-acceptance.md#stage-evidence-for-art-methods).
6. **Expand according to covered scope**: add relevant samples for new objects, scales, or relationships. Across the film, check whether abstract segments remain recognizable as the phenomenon, and check light/color, material, sound, and action joins. Finally watch and listen to the current encoding in full.

Across scales, preserve the same object's contour, position, orientation, or local features. Let viewers fully observe the necessary conditions, object change, and result of a key mechanism at least once. Present actual sequential/parallel relationships without fabricating delays. Place input and output side by side in the same scene when comparison requires it. In 3D, explain conditions and units through narration/synchronized captions; other media may use short text as actually needed.

In an authorized huashu project, retain the original engine and timeline, store finely painted assets in the episode directory, and composite through object-local transforms/masks. Keep scientific geometry and the event table inspectable. Retain asset IDs, action cards, and `qa.visual_frames`; see [tool routing](tool-routing.md#huashu-art-motion-execution) for dependencies. Set observation pauses and simplified backgrounds according to explanatory needs.

If the subject is too small on a phone for contact/side selection to be recognized, or a long stretch contains only diaphragms, waveforms, and labels, redesign shot size and whole-to-detail connections before refining textures.

## Preserve Recognition and Give the Topic Room

Present potentially disturbing subjects through restrained composition, lighting, and detail while preserving scientific identity and necessary risks. Do not automatically turn realistic main shots into cartoons. A gentle, cute, finely finished 2D style applies only within this episode's authorized 2D scope or to separate covers. It cannot remove structures required by the mechanism or imply that a harmful subject is harmless.

The applicable 2D/cover direction is fresh, bright, clear, and vividly colored natural whole scenes: clear blue sky, warm light, lively plant colors, natural object colors, and foreground/midground/background. People, environment, mechanisms, and text should appear to share one author. Close-ups may simplify without reverting to another black-background, thin-line teaching-slide style. Do not merely recolor old dark components to pale colors or substitute fluorescence, global halos, crude geometry, or texture everywhere for brightness.

Use colors by role: environment/air, subject, structure/shadow, focus, and text. Specific hex values/type scales/line widths live in parameter files; do not maintain a second numerical set in this text. Daylight 45/35/15/5 is only an adjustable visual-area starting point, not a pixel count of the original, and is not imposed on night, ocean, or other topics. Scientific colors, species features, spectral order, and variable-color semantics take priority. Add shape, line-style/text assistance where necessary; do not distinguish by color alone.

Refine actual assets through [three-scale finishing](#three-scale-form-and-production-detail), and diagnose gaps with [same-shot comparisons](#representative-samples-and-l2l3-calibration). See [production checkpoints](production-checkpoints.md#2-make-explanatory-relationships-visible) for action relationships and timing.

## Pure-2D Explanation and Real Assets

In `pure_2d` mode, explain principles through pure-2D illustration/animation, without substituting 3D models or pseudo-3D renders. Pure 2D constrains the production method; it does not require flat-filled objects. Two-dimensional painting, vector shading, edges, and materials may convey volume, transparency, reflections, and environmental reflected color, subject to scientific conditions and the currently approved style. When the user has locked `ai_generated`, develop continuous principle/phenomenon picture under [AI-generated video](ai-generated-video.md), still providing verifiable scientific simplification and disclosure and never pretending it is live action. Continuous live-action video serves only as a `real_footage` observation anchor. 2D may provide annotations, magnification, sections, or invisible mechanisms; a complete 2D explanation requires the user's specific approval for that scope. This paragraph cannot justify replacing realistic main picture. Acquisition, the default opening, and processing limits for real video belong to the [asset rules](science-and-rights.md#minimum-production-statement). A diagram cannot masquerade as scientific evidence, and reference-image/cover use cannot fill a live-action gap.

Design establishing, medium, and mechanism close-up views separately, preserving identity and scientific relationships rather than repeatedly enlarging one image. Light texture/gradients/shadows serve space and recognition only; unsupported volumetric lighting cannot hide weak form. If scientific colors, scale, time, or paths are simplified/processed, state the boundaries under the asset rules.

For explanatory shots, first establish necessary objects, references, and attention centers under [spatial relationships and audiovisual correspondence](production-checkpoints.md#match-each-sections-picture-sound-and-in-picture-text), then choose shot size, angle, and background. The approved style constrains form and representation; it does not require retaining the composition of one whole-scene image. Reconstruct a side view, detail view, or simplified environment as needed so background detail and contrast serve relationship recognition while object identity and scientific correspondence remain intact. Simplification may remove information this shot does not need. Preserve the currently approved style's finish for the redesigned shot size and object responsibility; simplification cannot justify reducing a subject to crude placeholder geometry. Do not preserve beautiful scenery by shrinking the explanatory objects to unrecognizable dots/lines with labels added, or attempt to rescue that with whole-frame shrinkage, excessive text, or unrelated decoration.

## Portrait Aspect Ratio and Composition

This section's title and 9:16 examples remain for restoring existing portrait work. General composition principles also apply to current 16:9 landscape work. See “Future Landscape Work and Moderate Approachable Treatment” below for future defaults and independent layout.

Organize a 9:16 scene across the complete frame, including its edges in the composition. Sky, water surfaces, solid-color environments, and other purposeful low-detail regions may all be part of a scene. Negative space separates objects, accommodates gaze or movement, emphasizes mechanisms, and holds necessary text. Do not fill empty space with decoration merely because it is empty. Its relationship to the subject and explanatory task must be explainable. Missing scenes, unsupported borders, and unused large title boards cannot pass as negative space. Distinguish real cosmic/shadow darkness from artificial black borders.

Choose centered, offset, diagonal, top/bottom, or comparison compositions according to object relationships in the shot. The geometric centerline is not a quality criterion; do not impose composition rules just for variety. Arrange gaze, motion direction, contact position, and attention centers in adjacent shots around the subject's contour and optical center of gravity. Compare rough thumbnails only when the choice is unclear. Lay out main picture, in-picture labels, captions, and platform overlays separately; do not shrink or shift the entire frame toward the upper left to avoid UI. Review guides must not enter the final film.

Do not stretch picture, crop scientific subjects/foot-contact points/contacts, or fabricate imagery to fill the frame. If an asset cannot be safely recomposed, replace it with a self-produced clip meeting the [original visual provenance contract](science-and-rights.md#original-visual-provenance-contract), or make a self-designed diagram within the authorized scope, without falsely claiming live-action requirements have been met. Organize titles, captions, and necessary local backings with the scene. Background detail serves the subject; do not use particles, texture, or enlarged backplates to fill every blank area.

## Text, Centering, and Motion

Use the approved font/weight, consistent alignment and margins, and clear space around the observation target. Necessary text must remain readable at phone size; tiny, ultra-thin or low-contrast type is not refinement. Improve placement and contrast first, adding restrained backing only when needed for permitted text. Captions normally use 1–2 semantic lines in the lower readable area; do not split terms or numbers from units.

Ordinary synchronized narration/accessibility captions remain an editable 2D layer at delivery resolution. In 3D picture, only necessary single-letter markers may accompany the objects; use actual editable [spatial letters](spatial-typography.md), never explanatory words, values/units or 2D plates. A matching 3D letter pass may reuse unaffected beauty frames; changes rerender the affected letter/light/depth dependencies. Other media retain their original text treatment. Do not enlarge text baked into a proxy. Design covers separately.

Generate caption backings from the actual font, font size, line spacing, and outer bounds of stroked glyphs plus necessary padding. Do not estimate from character counts or use a fixed large horizontal box; a short line must not receive a large two-line box. Keep text-layer anchors/final-line baselines stable. Backing width/height changes must not drag the text layer. The same sentence may cache its layout rather than recalculating every frame and jittering. If text is too wide, wrap/split the sentence first; do not shrink type to force it in or invade platform safe areas.

The current project's baseline/offset and once-applied revision marker live only in the [series profile](../config/series-profile.json). They are historical 1080×1920 revision settings, not a permanent universal Y position or platform safe area. On restoration, read the target from the applied marker; do not subtract the same offset again. For other resolutions at the same aspect ratio, scale by height and record rounding; redesign a changed aspect ratio. Translate only captions and their close-fitting backing. If they collide with necessary labels, apply the deletion test first, then make local avoidance adjustments while preserving target direction, font size, and interval; do not move the whole main picture. Record additional rearrangement honestly rather than calling it a pure pixel translation. Do not retrospectively alter published or approved publication-ready versions for this reason.

Design video object labels, locating lines, directional arrows, and area emphasis consistently under [precision technical annotations](annotation-style.md). Read defaults from the [separate landscape annotation parameters](../assets/style/precision-annotations.tokens.json), without carrying over historical portrait label font sizes or rounded backplates. The approved style may be reused; still inspect actual anchors, tips, and dynamics item by item. The [vector example](../assets/style/leader-and-arrow.svg) is an editable construction example, not evidence that the finished film passes. Continue to organize layers by their responsibilities: subject, necessary emphasis, leader lines/arrows, labels, and captions. Changing parameters does not automatically rerender existing assets; change actual source files, rerender, and update references.

### Body Auxiliary Text: Use and Hold

**No upper-left or upper-right scene descriptions in body shots.** This includes shot names, scene summaries, two-line headings, process-stage tags and recurring chapter headings. Do not move the same heading to another corner or restyle it as a capsule. Establish location and progression through the picture, narration and transition. A necessary chapter title or route overview belongs in a dedicated transition/overview composition and leaves before the body resumes; do not insert a title card for every shot. Process diagrams must preserve actual branches, repetitions and order.

Before adding an explanatory sentence, try to show its content through the subject's action/state, framing, a motivated scale transition or a meaningful comparison. Generic captions describing the view or model normally leave the image. If removing them makes the mechanism unclear, repair the visible event or [shot relationship](viewer-retention-design.md#express-relationships-through-shots-use-montage-as-needed) instead of restyling the explanation as a label. Preserve necessary qualifiers/conditions in synchronized narration or other existing appropriate channels; do not remove scientific uncertainty along with graphic text. For 3D videos, the [single-letter rule](spatial-typography.md) prohibits all explanatory picture text, including necessary-looking words/values and 3D plaques; only necessary single-letter markers remain. Ordinary synchronized subtitles stay 2D. Other media retain their existing text rules.

The following auxiliary phrase/value/condition uses apply to non-3D picture only. In 3D picture use a necessary single-letter marker or express the information through the actual narration and synchronized subtitles; this list does not create an exception. For the applicable non-3D scope, use auxiliary text only when removing it would lose information needed for the current observation:

- Identify a specific part or state that the viewer must locate or distinguish; place its short label beside the feature or bind it with a precise leader. A detached scene heading does not become an object label by renaming it.
- Show a necessary number/unit, scale, comparison, symbol/legend or concise relationship that cannot be read from the picture and synchronized captions alone.
- State a scientific condition needed to interpret the current result, using the shortest accurate wording. Attribution and disclosures follow their existing placement/permission rules.

Do not repeat the spoken sentence as a second caption, label an obvious scene, fill empty space, or replace a visible mechanism with prose. Use one short phrase or sentence, not a paragraph. Present one auxiliary reading task at a time; a necessary comparison may form one group. If it competes with the caption or a new visual event, sequence the information or rewrite the narration. Keep ordinary subtitles synchronized as complete semantic units; these auxiliary-label floors do not apply to each subtitle word.

Read the short-label and explanation/comparison minimums in `audience_text.auxiliary_reading` in [production policy](../config/production-policy.json). They are editorial floors, not a guarantee of comprehension or a fixed shot length. Extend for unfamiliar terms, multiple values, locating the referent and actual phone-size reading.

- Count only one **continuous, fully legible, stable reading interval**. Entrance/exit animation, occlusion, low-contrast frames and moving/reflowing text do not count. Target motion is acceptable while the text remains stable and the binding clear. Separate flashes cannot be added together; a substantive text change starts a new reading interval.
- Plan the complete text, referent, entrance, stable interval and exit in the existing action card/timeline before rendering. Keep the text visible through the relevant observation after the minimum. Live values may update under a stable descriptor; hold the important result long enough to read and compare, rather than freezing each intermediate digit.
- If a shot or visible target cannot support the reading interval, shorten/remove the auxiliary text, use the narration, or extend/replan the observation. Never leave a label pointing at an absent target merely to reach a duration. Do not compress a sentence into a brief flash to preserve a cut.

Review the final encode at normal speed and phone viewing size with the actual narration/captions: the viewer must read the text and find its referent without pausing or rewinding. In the existing overlay evidence/QA observation, record each auxiliary item's stable readable start/end and the reading/locating result; appearance timestamps alone do not establish dwell. A forbidden corner heading, a subminimum reading interval, or text understood only after pausing is a failure. Fix and recheck the affected sequence; metadata checks and still frames cannot approve temporal readability.

### Visible Text Purposes and Production Excuses

On-screen, caption, voiceover and cover text must serve the topic, object/mechanism, accessibility, scientific conditions or necessary attribution/license information; one item may have several real purposes. Shot-list names, working titles, generic scene-description captions, production-status notes, material defects, tool limits and internal QA belong in private records or appropriate companion material, not body-shot overlays, even briefly. For 3D picture specifically, the single-letter rule overrides auxiliary word/value/condition allowances: no explanatory words or numeric labels, including plaques, genuine lettering or scientific exceptions. Genuine synchronized narration/accessibility subtitles retain their 2D role and actual correspondence checks. Other media retain their existing purpose/necessity, scientific-condition and consent rules. Apply the deletion test before styling a label. When a real object/state needs identification, use a necessary single-letter marker in 3D picture; other media may use the shortest accurate term. If a qualification is necessary to avoid mistaking a teaching model for a specific real object, preserve that meaning at the relevant point in narration and genuine synchronized subtitles for 3D, or an appropriate concise cue in other media; do not merely rename or conceal the limitation. AI/simulation production disclosures default to platform fields and companion material, with specific on-frame consent handled below. In channels that permit words, no keyword, including one meaning “illustration” or “model,” is automatically forbidden; this does not permit words in 3D picture.

Edit defensive narration by purpose: remove material with no audience value, express a needed distinction as a direct positive explanation, and keep a genuinely necessary scientific condition where it is needed to understand the claim. For a structural tour followed by production, a clear transition such as “先看部件怎样组成整体，再沿制造过程看它怎样成形” can establish the viewing order. Do not automatically move discarded sentences into tiny corner notes; companion material is appropriate only when the information need not accompany the observation.

For example, “素材不足只能这样画” and “本片验收已通过” are production excuses. Remove them even if reworded or moved to narration/covers. Do not invent original sound when an asset has none. When a scientific task requires sound comparison, obtain suitable assets or adjust the supported expression.

Deletion does not mean concealing science or rights. For necessary source attribution, license information, and scientifically meaningful time scaling, experimental conditions, and limits on conclusions, check content obligations and placement separately. AI production disclosures default to platform fields and companion materials; handle in-picture conflicts through the specific-consent rules below. ### Connection Topology and Interface Continuity

When pipes, cables, linkages, or other connectors appear, first design correct object/port roles and connection topology. Specify diameter or cross-section changes, physical fittings, centerlines and mating/insertion depth, occlusion order, and endpoint identity throughout motion and across shots. Discontinuity, wrong-port connections, abrupt changes without reasonable physical fittings, floating open ends, or merely visual bridges are failures. Judge by connection type rather than mechanically requiring all lines to have equal thickness.

Confirm port function using reliable model-specific documentation, structural drawings, verifiable observation of the real object, or explicit teaching-model definitions. Do not assign inlets/outlets from color, appearance, or nearby position alone. Also check terminal tangents/centerline coaxiality, insertion, and fastening structures. If model or purpose evidence is missing, verify it first; do not sign an unknown port as correct.

Continue using each shot's `shotbook.connections`. First determine whether cross-component connections require review. A scene without connections records only `applicable=false`, a genuine `not_applicable_reason`, and empty `links`; do not invent fittings, thickness values, or reviews. When connections exist, use stable IDs to record each type, endpoint objects/ports and functional roles, cross-section/diameter-transition design, joint geometry, occlusion, and motion/cross-shot continuity. A diameter change needs a physical transition appropriate to the object. Cables may differ in thickness and linkages in cross-section, but their structures must be explained; background concealment cannot excuse an abrupt change. Changing endpoints or roles under the same connection ID requires an explicit design reason. If the scientific process includes disconnection/reconnection, show those states explicitly: do not misclassify genuine disconnection as an accident or permit unexplained reconnection.

During actual continuous viewing/listening of a candidate, compare each designed endpoint role with what is observed, viewing the overview, interface close-up, and key motion/cut states. Check centerlines, end-face contact/insertion depth, and whether pipe mouths/fittings really join. Bending, scaling, occlusion, or cuts must not create open breaks, exposed cheats, abrupt thickness changes, swapped depth order, or false connections. The same clear key-state comparison sheet may be reused; no fixed screenshot count or common diameter is required. Repair remaining interface issues in the corresponding connection/shot and review again. Caption explanations, thick covering lines, or passes in other shots cannot compensate.
